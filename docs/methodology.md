# Methodology — Meta's crawlers and news publishers' robots.txt

_This document is generated by `mcs.report.methodology` from a fixed template, `config/*.yaml` and (where available) the frozen panel metadata. Section 2 is the pre-registration and does not depend on any measured value._

## 1. What we measured

For news publishers and a general top-sites control group we read each site's robots.txt and recorded, for every crawler token below, whether the file blocks it. We compare Meta's five documented crawlers with other AI and search crawlers, track changes around Meta's updated Commercial Terms (effective 30 Oct 2026), and count publishers that show public signals of Meta business-tool use but do not block Meta's crawlers in robots.txt.

Crawler tokens evaluated (`config/bots.yaml`):

- **META_TOKENS**: `meta-externalagent`, `meta-externalads`, `meta-webindexer`, `meta-externalfetcher`, `facebookexternalhit`
- **LEGACY_META**: `facebookbot`, `facebot`, `facebookcatalog`
- **PEER_AI_TRAINING**: `gptbot`, `claudebot`, `anthropic-ai`, `google-extended`, `ccbot`, `bytespider`, `applebot-extended`, `cohere-ai`, `amazonbot`
- **PEER_AI_SEARCH**: `oai-searchbot`, `chatgpt-user`, `claude-searchbot`, `claude-user`, `perplexitybot`, `perplexity-user`
- **CLASSIC_SEARCH**: `googlebot`, `bingbot`

Meta's documentation describes `meta-externalads` as the crawler for "improving advertising and other business-related products and services". We call it *the crawler Meta describes for advertising and business products* and make no claim about how Meta actually uses it.

## 2. Pre-registration (fixed 19 Oct 2026, before the T2 live snapshot of 20 Oct 2026)

This section was written before any T2 data existed and is not edited after seeing the data.
The decision rules below are applied mechanically by `mcs.analyze.metrics.evaluate_hypotheses`.
A hypothesis is reported as **supported**, **not supported**, or **insufficient data**
(denominator below 20, or for H6 fewer than 10 changed domains).

**Null results are still stories.** Every hypothesis is reported whichever way it falls. A
"not supported" outcome is a finding, published with the same prominence as a "supported" one,
and the rules are not changed after the data are seen.

| ID | Hypothesis | Pre-registered decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Most news publishers (>50%) still do NOT block `meta-externalagent` days before 30 Oct. | Supported if the share of determinate news domains that do **not** `FULL_BLOCK` `meta-externalagent` is > 50%. |
| H2 | Few publishers (<5%) changed their Meta rules after 15 Oct. | Paired news domains (determinate at both T1 and the current snapshot; T0 if T1 is unavailable). Supported if the share whose verdict or naming changed for any `META_TOKENS` or `LEGACY_META` bot is < 5%. |
| H3 | `meta-externalads` is blocked far less often than `meta-externalagent` (the "ads gap"). | Supported if the `FULL_BLOCK` share for `meta-externalads` is lower than for `meta-externalagent` among news domains **and** either the two Wilson 95% CIs do not overlap or an exact McNemar test on the same domains gives p < 0.05 in that direction. |
| H4 | Publishers block OpenAI/Anthropic/Google training bots more often than Meta's (the "Meta gap"). | Supported if the share of news domains that `FULL_BLOCK` at least one of `gptbot`, `claudebot`, `google-extended` exceeds the `meta-externalagent` `FULL_BLOCK` share **and** the exact McNemar test on the same domains gives p < 0.05. |
| H5 | A large share of Meta-linked publishers are exposed. | "Large" means > 50%. Among news domains with E1, E2 or E2b signals, supported if > 50% do not `FULL_BLOCK` `meta-externalagent`. |
| H6 | Changes cluster by owner group or platform template. | Among news domains that changed any Meta rule (same comparison as H2), supported if > 50% share a template cluster (identical normalised robots.txt) or a known owner group with at least one other changed domain. Needs ≥ 10 changed domains. |

**Analysis plan.** Primary shares are unweighted, with Wilson 95% intervals; denominators are
domains with a determinate robots.txt outcome. News vs control differences use the Newcombe
hybrid score interval; paired before/after comparisons use the exact McNemar test with the
discordant counts b and c reported. A size-weighted share (weight = 1/log10(Tranco rank + 10))
is secondary and labelled as a traffic proxy. Segment metrics with a denominator below 20 are
suppressed ("n<20"). Every published number carries its n.

## 4. How the data were fetched

All robots.txt and ads.txt files were fetched with **our own direct HTTP client** (Python `httpx`), run from **GitHub Actions runners (Microsoft Azure data centres in the US)**. It exposes the upstream HTTP status and the final URL after redirects, which RFC 9309 semantics require. **No third-party scraping API was used — in particular, the data were not collected through HasData's Web Scraping API.** HasData publishes the study; the "Data: HasData" credit on charts refers to the publisher, not to the collection method. Challenge pages were not retried through any proxy service; they are recorded as `INDETERMINATE_BLOCKED`.

- **User-Agent:** `MetaCrawlerStudy/1.0 (+https://github.com/alexter123123123/research-meta)`. The repository README is the bot information page (purpose, contact via GitHub issues, opt-out).
- **Politeness:** at most 1 concurrent request per host, at least 2.0 s between requests to the same host, global concurrency ≤ 25; timeouts 10 s connect / 20 s read; at most 2 retries (network errors, 5xx, 429 only, honouring `Retry-After`); no cookies, no logins, no JavaScript for robots.txt/ads.txt.
- **robots.txt:** `https://{canonical host}/robots.txt` after following up to 5 redirects from the homepage; the apex host's file is also fetched for QA. Bodies are truncated at 512000 bytes (500 KiB, the RFC 9309 parsing limit). Every body is stored with its SHA-256 and a metadata sidecar.
- **Homepages (news panel, once):** only if our own robots.txt rules allow `/`; rendered with **headless Chromium (Playwright)** and also fetched as raw HTML, and both are scanned. If `/` is disallowed for us, we use the latest Wayback capture within 30 days instead.
- **DNS:** public TXT records of the registered domain. **GTM:** the public `gtm.js` container for any GTM ID on the homepage.
- **Baselines and history:** Internet Archive Wayback Machine CDX queries and raw `id_` captures (original bytes, no Wayback banner), at most 1 request/second, single connection, back-off from 30 s doubling to 600 s, cached on disk.
- **Snapshots:** T0 = latest capture in [1 Sep, 1 Oct 2026 23:59:59 UTC]; T1 = latest capture in [2 Oct, 14 Oct 2026 23:59:59 UTC] (T0 carried forward and flagged if none); T2 = live, 20 Oct 2026 from 06:00 UTC; daily live snapshots to 20 Nov 2026 (extended from 6 Nov); T3 = 29 Oct; T4 = 6 Nov. All times UTC.

### Fetch-outcome treatment (RFC 9309)

| `fetch_status` | Condition | Treatment in headline metrics |
|---|---|---|
| `OK` | 2xx, text or empty, parseable | Parsed |
| `OK_EMPTY` | 2xx, empty body | Allow-all |
| `UNAVAILABLE_4XX` | 400–499 except 429, after redirects | Allow-all (RFC 9309 §2.3.1.3); counted separately |
| `RATE_LIMITED_429` | 429 after retries | Indeterminate (Google treats 429 like 5xx) |
| `UNREACHABLE_5XX` | 500–599 after retries | RFC 9309 says assume full disallow; we treat it as **indeterminate** and report the count |
| `INVALID_HTML` / soft-404 | 2xx but an HTML page | Soft-404 with no rules: allow-all (flagged). Challenge page: indeterminate |
| `NETWORK_ERROR` | DNS/TLS/timeout after retries | Indeterminate |
| `INDETERMINATE_BLOCKED` | Bot-challenge page served to our client | Indeterminate |

## 5. Parsing rules

- Our own RFC 9309 group extractor; every verdict is cross-checked with Protego and disagreements are reviewed by hand.
- UTF-8 decoding with latin-1 fallback; BOM stripped; CRLF/CR/LF accepted; comments stripped; field names case-insensitive; a missing space after the colon is tolerated.
- Consecutive `user-agent` lines share a group; groups naming the same token are merged (RFC 9309 §2.2.1). Only `allow`/`disallow` are evaluated; an empty `Disallow:` means no restriction.
- Path matching supports `*` and `$`; the longest match wins, and `allow` wins a tie (RFC 9309 §2.2.2).
- **Token matching:** exact (`meta-externalagent`) and versioned/prefix (`meta-externalagent/1.1`, `meta-externalagent*`) count as *named*. Non-standard spellings (`Meta-External*`, `MetaExternalAgent`, `facebook*`) are recorded as `nonstandard`, are **not** counted as named, and are reported as a misconfiguration sub-finding. The `*` group applies only when no group names the bot.
- **Probe URLs:** `/`, `/news/` and up to three article URLs sampled from the T2 homepage (a synthetic `/2026/10/example-article-slug/` if none is found). The same probes are reused for every snapshot of a domain.
- **Cloudflare-managed blocks** (`# BEGIN Cloudflare Managed content` … `# END`) and `Content-Signal:` lines are detected; generator fingerprints (Yoast, Rank Math, WordPress VIP, Arc XP, Brightspot …) are recorded.

## 6. Verdict definitions

| Verdict | Definition |
|---|---|
| `FULL_BLOCK` | The bot is named (exact or versioned/prefix token) and **all** probe URLs are disallowed for it. Called an "explicit block" in copy. |
| `PARTIAL_BLOCK` | Named; some but not all probes disallowed. |
| `ALLOWED_EXPLICIT` | Named; no probe disallowed. |
| `WILDCARD_BLOCK` | Not named; the `*` group disallows all probes. |
| `WILDCARD_PARTIAL` | Not named; `*` disallows some probes. |
| `ALLOWED_DEFAULT` | Not named; `*` allows all probes, or there are no rules. |
| `INDETERMINATE` | The robots.txt fetch outcome was indeterminate (table above). |

"Effective block" = `FULL_BLOCK` ∪ `WILDCARD_BLOCK`. Restrictiveness order (least → most):
`ALLOWED_DEFAULT` < `ALLOWED_EXPLICIT` < `WILDCARD_PARTIAL` < `PARTIAL_BLOCK` <
`WILDCARD_BLOCK` < `FULL_BLOCK`.

## 7. Changes, attribution and timeline

- Comparisons over time use only domains with a determinate verdict at both endpoints (paired design); the paired n is always stated.
- **Attribution:** a change whose named Meta rule sits inside a Cloudflare-managed block in every version that names it is labelled `cf_managed`; a change involving a named rule outside such a block is `authored`; a change only to the `*` group is `unknown`. Identical same-day changes inside Cloudflare blocks across unrelated domains are treated as a Cloudflare default change, never as publisher reaction.
- **Template clusters:** domains whose normalised robots.txt (comments and `Sitemap:` lines removed) hashes identically form a cluster; changes are reported per domain and per cluster, joined with owner group.
- **Timeline (M11):** a Meta-related change is a robots.txt version whose verdict tuple over `META_TOKENS` + `LEGACY_META` differs from the previous version. It is dated at the first capture of the new version; the previous capture bounds the true change time, and the median bracket width is published. Event markers are labelled "per press reports".

## 8. Signals of Meta business-tool use

| Level | Signal |
|---|---|
| E1 (strong) | `facebook-domain-verification` meta tag on the homepage or DNS TXT record on the apex. A lower bound: verification by file upload is not discoverable. |
| E2 (medium) | Meta Pixel on the homepage (`fbevents.js`, `fbq('init', …)` or `facebook.com/tr?id=`), raw or rendered HTML. |
| E2b (medium) | A `facebook.com` line (Meta Audience Network) in ads.txt. |
| E2-GTM (weak) | No direct pixel, but the public GTM container references the pixel. |
| E3 (weak) | Links to Facebook pages / Instagram profiles (share, dialog, plugin, login, policy, help and event URLs excluded; group links flagged separately), `fb:pages` or `fb:app_id` meta tags. |

Shares are reported cumulatively (E1; E1∪E2∪E2b; …∪E2-GTM; any). These are public proxies only; they never show that a site is covered by Meta's terms.

## 9. Statistics

Wilson 95% intervals for proportions; Newcombe hybrid score interval (method 10) for news-vs-control differences; exact McNemar test (two-sided binomial on the discordant pairs b, c) for paired changes; size-weighted shares (weight = 1/log10(Tranco rank + 10), missing rank treated as 1,000,000) as a secondary, clearly labelled proxy. Segments with n < 20 are suppressed.

Sanity benchmarks: control top-1,000 naming of `meta-externalagent` vs knownagents.com (~19%); control top-5,000 `FULL_BLOCK` vs the 7 Sep 2026 Tranco-5k census (17.2%); Tier A US peer training-crawler blocking vs Axios's 102 publishers (~42%, directional). Deviations > 10 pp are investigated in `reports/qa_log.md` before data freeze.

- B1 Control top-1,000: any rule naming meta-externalagent (M2) vs knownagents.com: ours 13.2% (n=683), reference 19.0% → pass
- B2 Control top-5,000: meta-externalagent FULL_BLOCK (M1) vs 7 Sep 2026 Tranco-5k census: ours 8.4% (n=3533), reference 17.2% → pass
- B3 Tier A US: FULL_BLOCK of any peer AI-training crawler vs Axios 102 publishers (directional only): ours 83.8% (n=37), reference 42.0% → flag

## 10. Limitations

- robots.txt is a voluntary convention. Blocking in robots.txt is a request; Meta says `meta-externalfetcher` and `facebookexternalhit` may bypass it in some cases. We do not observe crawler behaviour.
- One fetch location (US data centres). Sites may serve different content by region, client or IP reputation; bot-challenge pages are counted as indeterminate.
- Wayback coverage is incomplete, especially for regional sites; baselines may be days old at the cutoff, and change dates are bracketed by capture times.
- Signals of Meta business-tool use are proxies (E1 is a lower bound; E3 is weak).
- Probe URLs approximate a site's content; a site that blocks only some sections is classed as a partial block.
- Panel sources (Press Gazette, Wikidata, Tranco) have their own coverage biases.

## 11. Ethics

- Only public, unauthenticated resources: robots.txt, ads.txt, homepages, public DNS, public GTM JavaScript and the Wayback Machine.
- We honour robots.txt for our own user-agent on homepage fetches. We never send Meta's, OpenAI's or any third party's crawler user-agent, never test whether firewalls block Meta's crawler, and never bypass paywalls, logins or forms.
- The public domain-level table covers Tier A (large brands) only; regional outlets appear as aggregates and owner groups unless the publisher decides otherwise.
- No legal conclusions are drawn.

## 13. Deviations from the study plan

Decided with the study owner during fieldwork; recorded as they happened.

1. **Fetch engine.** No HasData API access is available, so all fetches use our own direct
   `httpx` client (spec §6.3 already allows `fetch.robots_engine: direct`; it also exposes the
   upstream status and final URL that RFC 9309 semantics need). Homepages are rendered with
   headless Chromium (Playwright). The methodology must say this; outputs must not claim the
   data was fetched with HasData's API. Third-party fallback is off (`fetch.fallback_engine: none`);
   challenge pages are recorded as `INDETERMINATE_BLOCKED`.
2. **Bot identity.** `MetaCrawlerStudy/1.0 (+https://github.com/alexter123123123/research-meta)`.
   The repo README is the bot-info page (purpose, contact via GitHub issues, opt-out). No email.
3. **Runner.** Long jobs and the daily monitor run in GitHub Actions; the dev container cannot
   reach web.archive.org.
4. **Press Gazette UK list** publishes brand names, not domains; a curated brand→domain map is
   used (`source_list=pg_uk_top50_*`, mapping recorded in `data/panel/pg_uk_brand_map.csv`).
5. **Politeness incident.** On 19 Oct 2026 ~17:45 UTC one request was made to
   `pressgazette.co.uk/?s=...` before its robots.txt (`Disallow: /?s=*`) was read. Sitemaps are
   used since. Logged in `reports/qa_log.md`.
6. **Press Gazette fetch method.** The three ranking articles were fetched once with `curl`,
   using the study UA, before we knew Press Gazette returns 403 to Python HTTP clients. One
   further curl fetch was made after the 403 was seen. That later fetch is logged in
   `reports/qa_log.md` and was not repeated. The ranking data itself comes from the public
   Datawrapper CSVs behind the articles. The article HTML was used only to find the Datawrapper
   IDs, and that HTML is cached in `data/panel/sources/`. The June 2026 "any language" list
   could not be located: the sitemap index returned 403. Tier A therefore uses the US, UK and
   world-English Top 50 lists (September 2026 data).
7. **Wikidata.** query.wikidata.org's robots.txt disallows `/sparql`, and Wikimedia's UA policy
   rejected our client. The identical SPARQL was therefore run on QLever's public Wikidata
   mirror (qlever.dev). Index details are in `data/panel/wikidata_query.sparql`.
8. **Chain lists.** Wikipedia (`/w/api.php` is disallowed; `/wiki/` pages returned 403) was not
   used. `owner_group` comes from Wikidata P127/P749 only, so `independent/unknown` is large
   (~68% of Tier B). The H6 owner-group analysis is limited accordingly.
9. **Tranco.** robots.txt disallows `/api` and `/list/`, so the latest daily list was used
   (ID 467YX, published 18 Oct 2026), not a list chosen by date.
10. **Tier B quality filters** (applied before sampling):
    - excluded a batch-created content-farm network (owner "VUGA", 27 domains);
    - excluded foreign-ccTLD editions mislabelled US/UK (e.g. epochtimes.fr/.it/.pl).
    All exclusions are in `panel_exclusions.csv`.
11. **Tier A size** is 96 registered domains, below the 120–200 target, because the source
    lists overlap heavily.
12. **Credit line.** HasData confirmed (19 Oct 2026) that the "Data: HasData" credit on charts
    is acceptable, given that the methodology says the data was collected without HasData's API.
    Spokesperson for the factsheet: Roman Milyushkevich, Co-founder and CTO at HasData.
13. **T2 start time.** The scheduled 06:00 UTC trigger for T2 did not fire (GitHub dropped the
    scheduled event). T2 was started manually at 08:31 UTC on 20 Oct 2026, the same day and
    well inside the 12-hour window. The daily monitor now has an off-the-hour primary schedule
    (06:07 UTC) and a backup run at 07:37 UTC.
14. **Wayback coverage.** robots.txt captures inside the baseline windows exist for about 75% of
    Tier A domains but only about 13% of Tier B domains. Wayback rarely archives small sites'
    robots.txt. The before/after comparison against 14 Oct (H2) therefore rests on 136 news domains
    with a genuine T1 capture, mostly large brands. The Common Crawl fallback in the plan was not
    feasible in the time available: Common Crawl's robots.txt captures are not URL-indexed. The
    deadline analysis (T2→T3→T4) uses our own daily live snapshots of all 1,496 news domains.
15. **Tracking period extended.** On 22 Oct 2026 the study owner extended daily monitoring and the
    public tracker from 6 Nov to 20 Nov 2026. T3 (29 Oct) and T4 (6 Nov) are unchanged; daily
    snapshots after 6 Nov are labelled D2026MMDD.

## 14. Data-quality decisions made during QA (before data freeze)

These were made after manual verification (see `reports/qa_log.md`) and before the data freeze.
None of them changes a pre-registered hypothesis or its decision rule.

1. **Archived error pages are not robots.txt files.** A Wayback capture that is an HTML page or a
   401/403 response shows the Internet Archive's crawler being turned away, not the site's rules.
   Such captures are treated as indeterminate (`WAYBACK_HTML` / `WAYBACK_BLOCKED`). Baseline
   selection takes the latest *valid* capture in the window. Two false "changes" found in
   manual QA (washingtonpost.com, thetimes.com) were caused by such captures.
2. **Bot-challenge detection** was extended (e.g. "Verifying your device", "Checking your
   browser") and applied to every stored body at parse time.
3. **Carried-forward baselines are excluded from "after 15 Oct" comparisons.** Where a site had no
   Wayback capture between 2 and 14 Oct, its 1 Oct state is carried forward as T1
   (`t1_carried`), as the plan specifies. A change against a carried baseline may predate the
   15 Oct story, so carried rows are excluded from all T1→later change counts (M4, H2). The
   change against the 1 Oct baseline is still reported separately (T0→T2).
4. **Archive-restricted domains.** Some domains (e.g. theguardian.com) cannot be queried in the
   Wayback CDX API (HTTP 403). They are labelled `WAYBACK_RESTRICTED` and have no baseline.
5. **Persistent fetch failures.** A second fetch pass with a longer timeout was made for transient
   failures. Sites that keep refusing data-centre traffic or serve bot challenges stay
   indeterminate; we do not route around them.
