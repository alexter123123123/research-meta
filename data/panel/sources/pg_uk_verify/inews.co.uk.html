Why Putin’s shadow fleet now poses greater danger to UK

Cahal Milmo

Chief Reporter

“Spoofing” strategies are being deployed by Moscow for ships that frequently transit the Channel and Britain’s coast – some of which are known to carry weapons.

How are they doing this? 

  • New analysis suggests Russia is deepening its manipulation of the automatic identification system (AIS).
  • Russia is equipping its vessels with technology which interferes with the AIS system to make it look like the same ship is in two places – often hundreds of miles apart – at the same time.  
  • New analysis by Ollie Ballinger from UCL showed at least a dozen “shadow fleet” vessels – all but one sailing under a Russian flag – have used a version of the technique in recent weeks. 

What other spoofing techniques are being deployed? 

The growth in another technique, known as an AIS “handshake”, was revealed by The i Paper last month.  

A shadow fleet tanker sails close to another vessel and swaps AIS credentials. The tanker continues its journey while appearing to trackers as the fishing boat. 

Analysis

What is the risk to the UK? 

Any interference with the AIS system represents a significant navigation risk. In a report earlier this year, the Royal Institute of Navigation said such activity presents “a significant cyber security vulnerability and urgent risks to maritime safety”.

There are also fears that shadow tankers are being equipped with weapons or used as launch pads for surveillance drones.

Why up to 20 maternity units in England may close


Smaller maternity units delivering 1,500-2,000 babies a year are becoming less feasible because of a lack of obstetricians and a sharp rise in C-sections.

What you need to know

  • Matthew Hopkins, acute care director at NHS Alliance, said the current system is “not fit for purpose”.
  • As many as 20 of England’s 155 NHS maternity units could shut, he told The Guardian.
  • Some smaller units struggled to maintain safe staffing numbers.
  • Units in rural and coastal areas are most at risk.

Dog owners urged to desensitise pets to fireworks now

People should begin exposing their pets to the sound of fireworks now, in preparation for Bonfire Night, a charity has advised.

Guide Dogs has created a National Fireworks Archive to help.

Combatting anxiety

  • The archive is a free training video of fireworks footage to help owners gradually introduce their pets to the sounds.
  • Almost two thirds (63 per cent ) of the UK’s dogs are affected by fireworks anxiety, according to research by Guide Dogs.
  • However 79 per cent of dog owners say they do not do anything to prepare for Bonfire Night.

How can you prepare your dog?

Introduce gradually

Keep sessions short at about two to five minutes, once or twice a day to begin with.

Increase with caution

The volume can be increased only when the dog remains relaxed, with different sounds introduced carefully.

Make a safe space

Create a quiet, cosy den with blankets, toys and familiar scents, with treats hidden inside throughout the day.

What to do on the day

  • Owners should take their dog out for a final toilet break before dark.
  • Then close the curtains, turn on the TV or radio, prepare a food item and let the dog choose where to settle.

How to be more grateful for daily life in five easy steps

According to NHS GP Dr Radha Modgil

Gratitude

The world feels like a difficult place at the moment, and it can feel very hard sometimes to find things to be thankful for; when we are overwhelmed, we have less capacity to notice and reflect on the good things. Yet, ironically, proactively cultivating gratitude is one of the things that can actually help us feel less stressed.

NHS GP dR RADHA

The science

Research has shown that feeling or expressing thanks can actually alter our brain.

  • The balance of neurotransmitters change; more serotonin and dopamine (feel good chemicals) are produced.
  • This can be long lasting if we make gratitude a regular habit.
  • We also get a decrease in our cortisol (stress hormone) levels which helps lower anxiety.
  • Being grateful can strengthen the nerve pathways which triggers positive emotion.

How can we build gratitude?

Change your words

Instead of seeing something as a problem, try to see the opportunities within it. 

Journal

Our brains are very receptive and what we read or write can alter our emotional system.

Express it

This will not only make you feel good, but you will also get a positive response from others.

How can we build gratitude?

Notice more

Throughout your day, try to notice all the things that are going well; has someone opened a door for you, or smiled?

The basics

By being thankful for the small things every day, we are far more likely to be and stay healthy so that we can enjoy them.

What to do

Gratitude can lead to feelings of optimism, lower our blood pressure, and improve sleep quality. 

  • Write down what potential good aspects there are to the things you don’t feel like doing, or even what could be worse about it.
  • Spend a few minutes writing down three things that you are looking forward to that day, or the good things that have happened to you that day.
  • Say thank you to yourself for the things you have achieved, or managed to get through and changed that day.
